/ About

About Aloe Vera

What the plant is, how the leaf works, and where it comes from.

Mature Aloe vera plant with thick water-storing leaves

What is Aloe vera?

Aloe vera (Aloe barbadensis Miller) is a succulent plant in the family Asphodelaceae. It stores water in thick, fleshy leaves and is grown worldwide for cosmetic, traditional, and research uses. The clear gel inside the leaf is the part most people use on skin.

Gel versus latex

There are two different materials in the leaf:

  • Inner leaf gel — clear, almost all water, with polysaccharides, vitamins, and minerals. Used in most topical products.
  • Yellow latex — found under the green skin. Contains anthraquinones (such as aloin). It is a strong laxative and should not be used casually inside the body.
Tip: For face or burns, use only the clear gel and avoid the yellow layer.

A short history

Aloe appears in ancient Egyptian medical texts, including the Ebers Papyrus (around 1550 BCE). Greek writers such as Dioscorides described it for wounds and burns. In India it is known in Ayurveda as Kumari; in Chinese tradition as Lu Hui. Later it spread to the Americas and became a common houseplant and ingredient in modern skincare.

How it is grown today

Large farms grow Aloe in warm climates (for example Mexico, the Caribbean, parts of the United States, India, and China). Outer leaves are harvested by hand so the center of the plant can keep growing. Processing separates gel from latex when the product is meant for careful internal use.

Quality

Clear gel, clean preparation

Good topical use starts with clean leaves and clear gel only. That simple step improves comfort on sensitive skin.

Benefits of Aloe Vera

What is reasonably supported for skin and everyday care.

Skin comfort

Hydration

Skin and minor wounds

The best-known use of Aloe vera gel is on the skin. It helps keep the area moist, which supports comfort after sun exposure or small kitchen burns. It is not a substitute for medical care on deep burns, infected wounds, or serious injury.

After-sun and daily care

Many commercial after-sun products include Aloe because of its light texture and soothing feel. At home, a thin layer of fresh clear gel can be used after cleansing. People with very dry skin may prefer to add a drop of facial oil on top.

/ Uses

How to Use Aloe Vera

Practical steps for gel and clear limits for internal use.

Preparing fresh gel

  1. Choose a thick outer leaf and wash it well.
  2. Cut off the serrated edges.
  3. Stand the leaf upright for a few minutes so yellow latex can drain.
  4. Slice open and scoop only the clear gel.
  5. Use at once, or refrigerate in a clean jar for a few days.
Important: Do not put yellow latex on your face or in food or drink.

Common topical uses

  • Minor burns and sunburn (after cooling the skin with water)
  • Light moisturizer for face or hands
  • Scalp or hair mask before shampoo
  • After-shave comfort
  • Insect bite itch relief

Internal use — strict caution

Only use products made and labeled for internal use, preferably with clear “inner leaf” wording. Avoid latex-based products. Do not use internally if you are pregnant, breastfeeding, or giving it to young children, unless a doctor agrees. People on diabetes or heart medicines need professional advice first.

/ Care

Aloe Vera Care Guide

Light, water, soil, and simple troubleshooting.

☀️ Light

Bright, indirect light is best. Soft morning sun is fine; harsh midday sun can brown the leaves.

💧 Water

Water only when the top soil is completely dry. Overwatering causes soft, yellow leaves and rot.

🪴 Soil & pot

Use a fast-draining cactus mix. The pot must have drainage holes. Terracotta helps soil dry evenly.

🌡️ Temperature

Ideal range about 13–27°C. Protect from frost and cold drafts.

Propagation

Small side plants (pups) can be separated when they have a few leaves. Let the cut dry for a day or two, plant in dry mix, and wait about a week before light watering.

/ Recipes

Aloe Vera Recipes

Simple home formulas for skin and hair.

After-sun gel

½ cup clear gel + 1 tbsp rose water. Optional: a few drops lavender. Keep in the fridge. Use within a week.

Hair mask

¼ cup gel + 2 tbsp coconut or argan oil + 1 tbsp honey. Leave 45–60 minutes, then wash.

Calming face mask

3 tbsp gel + 1 tbsp plain yogurt. Apply 15–20 minutes, rinse. Use 1–2 times a week.

Overnight hands

2 tbsp gel + 1 tbsp honey + a little oil. Apply, wear cotton gloves overnight.

Safety for DIY

Use clean tools and jars. Fresh gel without preservatives spoils quickly — refrigerate and finish within several days. Patch-test new mixtures on the inner arm before using on the face. Dilute essential oils properly; more is not better.

/ Safety

Safety & Precautions

Use Aloe wisely — especially anything taken by mouth.

Topical use

Clear gel is usually well tolerated. If you have sensitive skin, do a patch test first. Stop use if redness or itching increases. Do not rely on Aloe alone for deep or infected wounds.

Internal use

Never drink or eat the yellow latex. Only consider products clearly made for internal use, and only after talking with a qualified professional. Avoid internal Aloe if you are pregnant, breastfeeding, or treating young children, unless a doctor says otherwise.

Who needs extra care

  • People with kidney or serious digestive disease
  • Anyone on diabetes medication, digoxin, or strong diuretics
  • Those scheduled for surgery (ask your care team about herbal products)
This website is for education only. It is not personal medical advice.
/ FAQ

FAQ

Short answers to common questions.

Can I put fresh gel on my face every day?

Many people do. If your skin is reactive, start every other day. Use only clear gel, no yellow latex.

How often should I water my plant?

Only when the top soil is dry. In winter that may be every few weeks. Less water is safer than more.

Is Aloe juice safe to drink?

Only products made for internal use, and only with medical advice. Avoid if pregnant or on certain medicines.

Why is my Aloe brown?

Soft brown = often overwatering. Dry brown tips = often underwatering or sun/cold stress. Adjust light and water.

Can I leave gel on overnight?

Yes. Pure gel works as an overnight layer. Add a drop of oil if it feels too tight or sticky.

Is Aloe safe for pets?

The latex is toxic to dogs and cats. Keep plants out of reach and ask a vet before any use on animals.

What to check on the label

  • Aloe position — higher on the list usually means more Aloe relative to fillers.
  • Fragrance & colour — extra scent or dye can irritate sensitive skin even if Aloe is present.
  • Bar vs liquid — bars often use oils and butters; liquids may list Aloe as a water-phase ingredient.
  • Claims — “with Aloe” is not the same as a medical treatment. Use soap for cleansing, not as a medicine.
Tip: If you have very dry or reactive skin, follow any Aloe soap with a light moisturiser so the skin barrier stays comfortable.

Choosing wisely

Aloe is a supportive ingredient, not a cure-all. For acne, eczema, or medical skin conditions, follow a clinician’s plan. For everyday comfort:

  • Prefer products that list Aloe barbadensis leaf juice or gel near the top when Aloe is the main reason you buy them.
  • Avoid high alcohol + fragrance mixes if your barrier is compromised.
  • Layer: cleanse → optional Aloe gel → cream if you need more occlusion.
